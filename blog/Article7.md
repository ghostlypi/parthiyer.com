# A computational analogy for the human brain

<span style="color: grey;">Published: 2026-04-20</span>

<details>
<summary>Description</summary>
A nice analogy to understand the human brain in the context of a computer.
</details>

The human brain is a very difficult entity to understand, but I find that
the current model for computation is a good analogy for the way the brain works.
Specifically, the rise of the ASIC (Application Specific Integrated Circuit),
SOC (System on Chip), and FPGA (Field Programmable Gate Array) have all accidentally
evolved into a similar form as that of our brain.

The best way to boil down the brain is to classify it as an FPGA. It is programmed
live as we learn new things and develop capability for specific tasks. But you may notice
that human conscience is not able to directly control all aspects of our bodies. One of the
most notable examples of this is heartbeat. We cannot conciously control our heartbeat,
at least directly. While there are levers we can pull to relax and stress ourselves, we can't
directly control our heart. While our heart is physically controlled to support our actions, there is a clear
layer of software abstraction between the human conscience and the physical behavior of out bodies.

I'd like to liken the human conscience as a program running on a specific accelerator, the prefrontal cortex, akin to an 
Artificial Intelligence Model running on a GPU. This portion of our existence is but a small part of our brain,
accounting for only 30% of our brain. But naturally, we still experience things beyond our direct conscience.
